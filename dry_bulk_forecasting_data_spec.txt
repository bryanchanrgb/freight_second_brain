# Dry-Bulk Commodity Price, Freight, and Market-Outlook Data Specification

**Version:** 0.2
**Prepared:** 2026-09-07 UTC
**Revision:** Added the public qualitative-news claims pipeline, evidence-graph schema, source-independence controls, contradiction handling, freshness rules, and prototype research results.
**Purpose:** Define a comprehensive, provenance-aware data system for forecasting dry-bulk commodity prices, freight rates, and producing qualitative market outlooks from news, research, and physical-market signals.
**Audience:** Human researchers, data engineers, quantitative analysts, and AI agents implementing or extending the system.

---

## 1. Scope and design principles

The system should forecast at least four distinct target families:

1. **Commodity prices:** iron ore, metallurgical/thermal coal, grains/oilseeds, bauxite/alumina, fertilizer and other dry-bulk commodities.
2. **Freight rates:** BDI, BCI, BPI, BSI, BHSI, route assessments, time-charter equivalents, and voyage rates.
3. **Forward market expectations:** FFA/futures curves, options where available, and broker/analyst forecasts.
4. **Qualitative outlooks:** structured, cited assessments of supply, demand, fleet, geopolitics, weather, regulation, and market regime.

The implementation must:

- Preserve the original publication timestamp and later revisions.
- Separate realized observations, assessments, forecasts, and narratives.
- Avoid look-ahead bias by using data vintages available at the forecast time.
- Record units, currency, route/vessel definitions, methodology versions, and licensing.
- Distinguish primary data from derived data and model-generated interpretation.
- Store raw immutable snapshots before normalization.
- Make every qualitative claim traceable to a source and evidence excerpt.
- Prefer APIs, bulk files, CSV/XLSX, RSS, and stable report URLs over browser scraping.

This is a market-research and forecasting system, not investment advice.

## 2. Proposed target taxonomy

### 2.1 Freight targets

Create separate daily series for:

- `BDI`: Baltic Dry Index.
- `BCI`: Baltic Capesize Index.
- `BPI`: Baltic Panamax Index.
- `BSI`: Baltic Supramax Index.
- `BHSI`: Baltic Handysize Index.
- Capesize 5TC and route series: C2, C3, C5, C7, C8_182, C9_182, C10_182, C14_182, C16_182, C17.
- Panamax 5TC and route series: P1A_82, P2A_82, P3A_82, P4_82, P5_82, P6_82, P7, P8.
- Supramax 10TC/11TC and route series: S1B, S1C, S2, S3, S4A, S4B, S5, S8, S9, S10, S15.
- Handysize 7TC and route series where licensed.

Baltic documents define the BDI as 40% Capesize, 30% Panamax and 30% Supramax and publish vessel assumptions and route formulas.[1][2]

### 2.2 Commodity targets

Recommended initial commodities:

- Iron ore: 62% Fe benchmark, fines, pellets/concentrate where available.
- Metallurgical coal and thermal coal.
- Wheat, corn/maize, barley, rice, soybeans and soybean meal.
- Bauxite and alumina.
- Phosphate rock and fertilizer products.
- Cement/clinker and selected minor bulks.

Commodity targets must include benchmark name, quotation location, grade/specification, currency, unit, assessment time, and whether spot, futures settlement, index, or forecast.

## 3. Source register

Status labels:

- **Verified public:** source page successfully retrieved and access method confirmed.
- **Verified licensed:** public product page confirms the product, but data requires account, subscription, exchange membership, or license.
- **Verified partial:** public page works, but full machine access or history is restricted.
- **Fallback:** useful substitute or open proxy, not equivalent to primary data.

## 4. Historical-record availability audit

This audit answers a specific question for every source in the source register: **does the source provide observations at multiple historical points in time, rather than only a current snapshot, product description, or forward-looking report?** The answer is recorded as `yes`, `partial`, or `no/primarily current`. Historical availability does not imply that the history is free, complete, revision-free, or legally redistributable.

### 4.5 Historical availability conclusion

The main sources without a clean, freely downloadable historical series are direct Baltic granular data, exchange historical settlements, AIS, Clarksons/S&P/Argus/Bloomberg data, and full subscription research. They do not lack historical information; they lack unrestricted access. The preferred remedy is a licensed feed or official export. For a public-data MVP, use the verified historical substitutes: Mendeley BDI, Trading Economics/Investing.com exports, World Bank/IMF XLSX, EIA API, USDA PSD vintages, FAOSTAT, UN Comtrade, UNCTAD/Data360 CSV, USGS reports, the Zenodo mine panel, FRED, public report archives, and Hellenic RSS.

### 4.6 Historical-data acceptance tests

A source should not be marked “historically usable” until the ingestion test confirms:

- At least two non-identical observation dates can be retrieved.
- The returned data includes a date/period field and a stable series/instrument identifier.
- Historical coverage start/end and frequency are recorded.
- The source distinguishes observation date from publication/revision date where applicable.
- A second retrieval produces an identical content hash or a documented revision.
- The license permits the intended storage, modeling and derived-feature use.

For forecast sources, add the stronger test:

- The original forecast can be stored with its publication timestamp and later matched to the realized target without replacing the original vintage.

Required graph nodes:

- `Source`: publisher, URL, source type, access status, quality, publication/retrieval times, content hash, license and upstream lineage.
- `Claim`: verbatim or minimally edited text plus normalized fields above.
- `Entity`: canonical index, vessel class, commodity, country, port, route, company or event ID with aliases.
- `Forecast`: target, target period, horizon, forecast value/range/direction, assumptions, scenario and source vintage.
- `Observation`: realized rate, index, commodity, trade, event or market-state measurement.
- `Event`: disruption, demand/supply change, policy, weather or routing event with start/end, affected entities and mechanism.


Keep current observations, forecasts, explanations, market-implied expectations and later outcomes as distinct claim/target types. A source count is not an independent-evidence count.

### 5.8 Source independence, deduplication and contradiction rules

Deduplicate by canonical URL, publisher, title/date, shared attribution, shared numerical values, text similarity and common event identity. Preserve lineage records even when claims are grouped. Repeated reports of one original market move must not increase corroboration as if they were independent sources.

Create an `independence_group` for each underlying information origin. A report that cites Bloomberg, Clarksons, IEA or another research publisher retains that upstream lineage and is not counted as independent evidence from each cited provider unless the upstream source was separately retrieved.

Only compare claims for contradiction after aligning:

- target and vessel class;
- commodity and route/geography;
- observation versus forecast versus explanation;
- publication vintage;
- forecast horizon and target period;
- units and magnitude definition.

Contradiction types must distinguish:

- **Direction:** bullish versus bearish for the same aligned target/horizon.
- **Horizon:** short-term observation versus medium-term forecast.
- **Magnitude:** direction agrees but numeric expectations differ.
- **Scope:** Capesize versus Panamax, or one geography/commodity versus another.
- **Internal tension:** one source reports positive momentum but neutral fundamentals.
- **Outcome reversal:** later realized value moves against the earlier signal.

Preserve unresolved disagreement. Do not average contradictory claims into an unsupported consensus.

### 5.9 News freshness and forecast-vintage rules

Use separate freshness classes:

```text
current: 0-7 days
recent: 8-30 days
aging: 31-90 days
historical_vintage: >90 days or forecast period completed
post_cutoff_outcome: published after the information cutoff
```

For a current evidence canvas, default to current and recent claims. Historical vintages remain valuable for source calibration, forecast evaluation and analogues but must not be presented as current evidence. Post-cutoff observations are outcome records only and must not enter the original forecast information set.

Every research run must store:

```text
run_id
scope_start
scope_end
information_cutoff
query_set
source_registry_version
parser_versions
raw_snapshot_manifest
claim_count
independent_group_count
contradiction_group_count
```

## 8. Forecasting and qualitative-outlook system design

### 8.1 Forecast horizons

- Nowcast: intraday/daily to one week.
- Short term: 2–8 weeks.
- Medium term: 3–12 months.
- Structural: 1–5 years.

### 8.2 Baseline models

Start with transparent baselines before deep learning:

- Seasonal naive and drift models.
- ARIMA/ETS and dynamic regression.
- VAR or Bayesian VAR for commodity/freight/macro interactions.
- Gradient-boosted trees with lagged, rolling and event features.
- Quantile regression for predictive intervals.
- Regime-switching models for high/low freight states.
- Hierarchical models across vessel classes and routes.
- Sequence models/Temporal Fusion Transformer only after robust baselines.

### 8.3 Validation

- Walk-forward evaluation only.
- Nested time-series validation for feature selection.
- Compare to seasonal naive, FFA implied rate and analyst consensus.
- Separate normal, disruption and regime-shift periods.
- Metrics: MAE, RMSE, MAPE where meaningful, pinball loss, CRPS, directional accuracy and calibration.
- Evaluate forecast vintages using only information available at issuance time.
- Test economic usefulness separately from statistical accuracy.

### 8.4 Qualitative outlook generation

The qualitative system should not simply summarize news. It should:

1. Retrieve fresh primary data, reports, news and market observations.
2. Cluster evidence by factor: supply, demand, fleet, route, cost, policy, weather, geopolitics.
3. Deduplicate articles and identify corroboration.
4. Extract evidence and publication dates.
5. Assign direction, magnitude, horizon and confidence.
6. Compare signals against price/freight action and market-implied expectations.
7. Produce base, upside and downside narratives.
8. State what would invalidate each scenario.

Required output:

```text
as_of
market_scope
current_regime
base_case
bull_case
bear_case
key_drivers
counter_drivers
leading_indicators_to_watch
catalysts
risks
confidence
citations
```

### 8.5 AI/LLM role

LLMs should be used for:

- News extraction and event normalization.
- Report table/paragraph extraction.
- Entity and route resolution.
- Evidence-grounded narrative synthesis.
- Hypothesis generation and feature documentation.
- Anomaly explanation.

LLMs should not silently invent numerical forecasts or overwrite measured data. Numerical forecasts must come from versioned model runs, with the LLM explaining—not fabricating—the output.

## 14. Public-data extraction run and reproducible pipeline notes

### 14.1 Implemented pipeline

An executable preparation pipeline was implemented at:

```text
/opt/data/dry_bulk_data_pipeline.py
```

It writes to:

```text
/opt/data/dry_bulk_data/
  raw/
  normalized/observations.csv
  metadata/latest.json
  metadata/quality_report.json
```

The pipeline performs no model fitting. It downloads public source files, stores immutable raw responses, records retrieval time, HTTP status, content type, byte count and SHA-256 hash, and writes a common long-form observation table with:

```text
observation_date, series_id, value, unit, source_id,
publication_time, vintage_time
```

The pipeline uses bounded retries with exponential backoff for transient HTTP errors, closes each response before moving to the next source, throttles Comtrade requests, and streams large USDA records into normalized output rather than retaining the full filtered result in memory. A run is labelled `complete` only when every configured request succeeds and at least one normalized observation is produced.

### 14.2 Successful extraction run

The verified run completed on **2026-09-05 02:58:04 UTC**:

```text
status: complete
requests: 28
successful requests: 28
failed requests: 0
normalized rows: 1,116,833
normalized source IDs: 8
normalized series IDs: 24,752
```

Artifacts are stored under `/opt/data/dry_bulk_data/`. The run manifest is `metadata/latest.json`; the quality report is `metadata/quality_report.json`.

### 14.3 Earlier errors and resolutions

The first run was interrupted during a Hermes gateway restart and also exposed source/pipeline issues. They were resolved as follows:

| Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Gateway restart interrupted the long-running command | Rerun as a bounded background process with artifacts and a final manifest; do not treat partial output as complete. |
| FRED read timeouts / HTTP/2 instability | Use an explicit User-Agent, `Connection: close`, bounded retries, exponential backoff, and a longer per-request timeout. |
| Comtrade HTTP 429 | Request years sequentially with retry handling and a 1.2-second delay between requests. |

## 15. Academic and commercial forecasting research audit

### 15.1 Literature reviewed

The research review covered recent systematic literature, open-access BDI studies, empirical shipping-market models, Panamax market research, technical-analysis/market-psychology work, and commercial freight-forecast products. The most useful synthesis is the 2026 systematic review by Kjeldsberg, Munim and Schramm: it reviews 28 freight-rate ML studies published from 2012–2024, identifies 17 target variables and 59 explanatory variables, and finds that 61% of studies focus on dry bulk, with weekly and daily data dominating.[25]

### 15.2 Methods not yet sufficiently represented in the base design

The project should test these methods in addition to ordinary ARIMA/ETS, VAR, gradient boosting and sequence models:

1. **Structural/empirical shipping-market models.** Model cargo demand, effective fleet, fleet utilization, scrapping, deliveries, speed and freight rates as an interacting system rather than treating the rate as an isolated time series. The 2025 *An Empirical Model of the Dry-Bulk Shipping Market* introduces a VAR-based market model and reports forecasting gains over simpler alternatives.[26]
2. **State-dependent and quantile causality.** Test whether commodity prices predict freight only in particular quantiles/regimes. Bandyopadhyay and Rajib use a nonparametric causality-in-quantiles approach and find asymmetric relationships between commodity spot prices and BDI.[27]
3. **Behavioral/technical-analysis layer.** Add moving-average crossovers, momentum, oscillators, trend persistence, turning-point labels, cycle phase and orderbook response. Mileski et al. argue that freight models relying only on fundamentals miss market psychology and capacity overshoot.[28]
4. **Time-frequency decomposition.** Evaluate EMD, wavelets, VMD/MVMD, spectral decomposition and multi-resolution models before and after regime changes. Prior BDI studies use EMD/wavelets; the 2025 MMAS paper combines MVMD, deep models and SHAP with external uncertainty factors.[29][30]
5. **Volatility and risk forecasts.** Add GARCH, GARCH-X, GARCH-MIDAS, VAR-BEKK-GARCH-X, quantile regression, EVT and conformal/predictive-interval methods. The target should include volatility, tail loss and probability of a rate move, not only point forecasts.[31]
6. **Forecast combinations.** Combine econometric, technical, ML, futures-implied and analyst forecasts. Research repeatedly finds hybrid/specialized combinations can outperform single models; compare combination weights out of sample rather than selecting one winner.[25][29]
7. **Small-data AutoML with lag-availability controls.** AutoML is promising for low-frequency shipping data, but every feature must be lagged to its publication availability. A recent maritime AutoML study used 43 explanatory variables and explicitly lagged unavailable variables.[32]
8. **Hierarchical/multi-task forecasting.** Jointly forecast BDI, BCI, BPI, BSI, route rates and commodity flows, sharing information while allowing segment-specific coefficients. This addresses the literature finding that sub-indices can be more informative for specific economic variables than the composite BDI.[30]
9. **Event-conditioned forecasting.** Train separate or mixture-of-experts models for normal, disruption, pandemic, war, canal-closure, crop-shock and mine-disruption regimes. External events can contain predictive information at multiple time scales, especially during volatility.[29]
10. **Market-implied versus model-implied curves.** Compare model curves with FFA/futures curves and measure forecast value as both statistical accuracy and improvement over the forward market. Commercial practitioners describe proprietary ML curves as inputs to fixing, hedging, fleet decisions and cargo exposure, not merely as a replacement forecast.[33]

### 15.4 Research-informed feature additions

Add these feature groups to the feature store:

- **DBECI-style macro composite:** EUR/USD, CNY/USD, Brent, US short rate, US term spread, commodity prices and market indices.
- **Technical state:** moving averages, rate-of-change, RSI-like oscillators, breakouts, drawdowns, trend persistence and turning-point labels.
- **Multi-resolution components:** low-frequency trend, seasonal component, high-frequency residual, wavelet/VMD/EMD components and reconstruction error.
- **Effective fleet:** nominal DWT adjusted for slow steaming, port congestion, scrubber/retrofit downtime, ballast inefficiency and route diversion.
- **Expectation gap:** model forecast minus FFA curve, analyst forecast, prior forecast vintage and realized rate.
- **Forward liquidity:** bid/ask, volume, open interest, curve slope, basis volatility and price-impact features where available.
- **Event shock variables:** binary and continuous indicators for canal/route disruptions, mine outages, crop events, policy shocks, sanctions and extreme weather.
- **Narrative consensus:** corroboration count, source-quality-weighted polarity, disagreement, novelty, expected horizon and revision rate.

### 15.5 Research design changes required

- Benchmark every new method against seasonal-naive, ARIMA/ETS, VARX, FFA-implied and analyst-consensus baselines.
- Use rolling-origin evaluation and publication-time feature lags; do not use revised trade data or later news in earlier predictions.
- Test daily, weekly and monthly frequencies separately; do not assume a model that wins at weekly frequency wins intraday or monthly.
- Report point accuracy, interval calibration, directional accuracy, turning-point detection, tail-risk performance and economic value after transaction/hedging costs.
- Test regime-specific performance: ordinary market, crisis, pandemic, war, canal disruption, commodity shock and supply-glut periods.
- Preserve negative results. The literature is vulnerable to data-snooping because many studies compare many models on one historical sample.
- Use SHAP/feature attribution only as descriptive interpretation; do not treat feature importance as causal evidence.

## 16. Public qualitative-news and claims-extraction prototype

### 16.1 Completed research run

A bounded public-source research run was completed for BDI-related news, analysis, predictions and public data sources.

```text
run_id: bdi_claims_prototype_20260905
scope: 2026-08-01 through 2026-09-05 UTC
information_cutoff: 2026-09-05T00:00:00Z
post_cutoff_outcome_check: 2026-09-07
raw source records: 471
unique canonical source URLs: 45
raw claim records: 200
complete claims before deduplication: 63
incomplete/title-only records: 137
explicit contradiction groups: 6
```

Artifacts are retained in:

```text
/opt/data/bdi_claims_prototype/
```

The production-facing subset is `claims_clean.jsonl`; `claims_incomplete.jsonl` and `claims_duplicates.jsonl` preserve audit records. `research_summary.md` contains the run findings and learned schema. The prototype is not a backtest and does not establish forecast accuracy.

### 16.2 Source classes used

The run searched and extracted from:

- Public market-data pages: Trading Economics, OilPriceAPI and public BDI article pages.
- Official benchmark/methodology: Baltic Exchange dry services.
- Public historical target data: Mendeley BCI/BPI/BSI/BHSI dataset.
- Dry-bulk trade press: Hellenic Shipping News, Seatrade Maritime News, Splash247, MarineLink and TradeWinds discovery results.
- Public research and industry analysis: Breakwave Advisors, Veson Nautical, MSI-linked public commentary, Drewry-linked public commentary and BIMCO-linked public commentary.
- Official/data discovery sources: UNCTAD, NOAA, World Bank, Reuters discovery pages and public API documentation.

A source found only through a search snippet or an inaccessible subscription page must be labelled `retrieval_status=search_snippet` or `public_summary`; it must not be treated as fully verified page evidence.

### 16.3 Research and processing method

The repeatable workflow is:

1. Define target, vessel class, commodity, route, geography, horizon and information cutoff.
2. Search separate source classes for observations, explanations, forecasts, risks, supply/demand, events and target-data sources.
3. Retrieve the page/report where legally accessible and record canonical URL, publisher, publication time, retrieval time, access status and source type.
4. Snapshot raw source content before LLM processing and retain content hash, parser version and raw object URI.
5. Extract atomic claims rather than article summaries.
6. Resolve aliases into canonical entities such as `BDI`, `BCI`, `BPI`, `BSI`, `iron_ore`, `coal`, `Capesize`, `Panamax`, `Simandou`, China and route concepts, while retaining the source wording.
7. Classify claim type, polarity, magnitude, forecast horizon, confidence and provenance.
8. Trace upstream attribution and assign `independence_group` so syndicated reporting is not counted as independent corroboration.
9. Deduplicate sources and claims using canonical URLs, title/date, shared numerical values, shared attribution, text similarity and event identity; retain lineage even when grouped.
10. Detect contradictions only after aligning target, segment, geography, commodity, horizon, publication vintage, units and claim type.
11. Preserve unresolved disagreement, internal signal tension and complementary segment views instead of averaging them into a consensus.
12. Separate current/recent evidence from historical forecast vintages and post-cutoff outcomes.
13. Link later realized observations to earlier forecasts through `forecast_outcomes` without allowing look-ahead leakage.

### 16.4 Learned findings from the run

The public evidence was not a single aggregate bullish or bearish view.

- At the September 5 information cutoff, public market pages reported strongly positive BDI momentum and a BDI level of 3,628 on September 4.
- Breakwave separated positive momentum from neutral fundamentals while maintaining an optimistic near-term Capesize view.
- Public MSI commentary described Capesize as stronger than sub-Capesize segments, with iron ore/bauxite support and Panamax supply pressure.
- Earlier forecasts were constructive on iron ore, Simandou, bauxite and longer-haul tonne-miles but more cautious on aggregate balance because of coal weakness, fleet growth, utilization and China uncertainty.
- Risks included Hormuz/geopolitical disruption, El Niño, grain yields, Chinese demand and trade policy.
- Repeated daily market reports often reflected one underlying market event; article count must not be treated as evidence count.

The central unresolved research question was:

> Can Capesize strength and longer-haul iron-ore/tonne-mile support outweigh coal weakness and fleet growth at the aggregate BDI level?

### 16.5 Contradiction groups observed

The prototype identified:

- Forecast horizon/magnitude differences within a single forecast page.
- Cutoff-versus-outcome reversal: BDI 3,628 near the cutoff versus 3,575 on September 7.
- Momentum versus fundamentals as separate signal layers.
- Capesize strength versus Panamax/sub-Capesize weakness as segment scope, not necessarily aggregate contradiction.
- Unresolved disagreement across MSI, BIMCO, Drewry and Veson on aggregate balance.
- Internal Breakwave tension between positive momentum, neutral fundamentals and optimistic near-term Capesize expectations.

### 16.6 Freshness and current-view policy

The earlier prototype mixed current evidence with old forecast vintages. The production pipeline must enforce the following:

```text
current: 0-7 days
recent: 8-30 days
aging: 31-90 days
historical_vintage: >90 days or forecast period completed
post_cutoff_outcome: publication after information cutoff
```

The current evidence canvas should default to `current` and `recent`. Older material should appear in a forecast-vintage/calibration panel. Post-cutoff material may be used for outcome evaluation only.

### 16.7 Public-data and qualitative-layer integration

The quantitative extraction and qualitative claims corpus are complementary:

```text
Quantitative extraction:
  FRED WTI, industrial production, EUR/USD and VIX
  USDA PSD commodity fundamentals
  UN Comtrade China iron ore and coal imports
  World Bank Pink Sheet commodity workbook
  Mendeley daily BCI/BPI/BSI/BHSI history

Qualitative extraction:
  current BDI observations
  BDI forecasts and market explanations
  Capesize/Panamax segment views
  supply/demand and fleet narratives
  geopolitical, weather, grain and policy risks
  source disagreement and contradiction groups
```

Claims must be linked to numerical observations where possible. For example, a claim about China iron-ore demand should link to the relevant Comtrade series, while a claim about BDI momentum should link to a versioned freight-rate series. Textual claims remain distinct from the numerical feature but can generate event, sentiment, expectation-gap and regime features.

### 16.8 Implementation requirements for the AI layer

The LLM/agent should operate through domain tools rather than unrestricted answer generation:

- `search_curated_sources(target, horizon, cutoff)`
- `retrieve_source(source_id)`
- `extract_claims(source_id, parser_version)`
- `resolve_entities(claim_ids)`
- `find_duplicates(claim_ids)`
- `find_contradictions(target, horizon, cutoff)`
- `get_market_snapshot(target, as_of)`
- `link_claims_to_series(claim_ids, series_ids)`
- `run_scenario(assumptions, target, horizon)`
- `evaluate_forecast_vintage(forecast_id)`
- `save_user_feedback(claim_or_group_id, adjustment, reason)`

Every generated answer should expose claim IDs, source URLs, freshness, provenance, independent evidence count, contradiction groups and whether a statement is source fact or agent synthesis.

### 16.9 Required next implementation step

Replace the manually seeded prototype claims with a repeatable extractor that:

1. Uses the source registry and curated query set.
2. Stores immutable raw pages/PDFs and exact evidence excerpts.
3. Enforces the information cutoff during retrieval and processing.
4. Produces complete claim records or explicitly marks extraction failure.
5. Deduplicates at source and claim level while retaining lineage.
